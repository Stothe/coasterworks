# Plan E: Coaster Builder Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Steve draws a coaster of his own: he lays a station, builds track piece by piece or by dropping points on the ground and in the air, sees bad stretches turn red with a reason, closes the circuit, runs a test, reads its excitement, intensity, nausea and G forces, opens it, and watches trains full of guests go round. Agents do the same through `coaster_build_from_points` and the piece commands. Designs save to `.track` files and load into any park.

**Architecture:** Track is data in the sim: an ordered list of pieces, each converted to a run of fixed-point segments (length, heading, pitch and roll at each end, element kind) and integrated with integer sine/cosine tables, so the same track gives the same positions on every engine. `packages/content` gains coaster families (limits, speeds, friction, car size, cost per piece, support style, model ids). A coaster is a ride record of kind `coaster` whose shape lives in `World.tracks` (by ride id); its station's entrance and exit use the D2 queue, loading platform and boarding code unchanged. A coaster system runs trains along the track with integer energy physics (gravity from pitch, rolling friction, air drag, lift chain, brakes, station dispatch), detects stalls and valley oscillation, and records the stats a test run needs; ratings come from those stats with original formulas. Point-driven building fits pieces through the points within the family's limits and explains every refusal. Snapshots carry each coaster's sampled centre line when its ride version changes and every train's position every frame; the renderer extrudes rails, ties, spine and supports in the modern look and draws trains instanced. The web app gains a Coasters chip in the Rides tray, a builder with draw and piece modes, and a coaster inspector with stats and a G-force strip.

**Tech Stack:** as before (TypeScript, Zod 4, Vitest, three.js, React 19, Playwright).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-coasterworks-design.md` (4.4 Rides tray chips, 4.5 Coaster builder, 4.6 Roller coasters and Ride operation, 4.7 content, 5.3, 5.5, 6.1) and the modern-look spec.

**Spec refinements made here:**
- Two original families to start, generic names: **Steel Coaster** (tubular rails on a round spine, column supports, tight curves, banking to 60°, no inversions in this plan) and **Wooden Coaster** (flat rails on timber, lattice bents, wider curves, banking to 30°, rougher ride). Inverted, looping, mine train, wild mouse and the rest are later data files; loops and other inversions (the spec's "picked elements") are Plan E2.
- Track lives on a grid like the rest of the park: each piece starts and ends on a tile edge centre at a whole height step, facing one of the four directions, with pitch from a small set (flat, gentle ±1 step per tile, steep ±2, vertical not yet) and bank from (none, gentle, steep) — so pieces join exactly and collisions are tile-based. Inside a piece the shape is a smooth curve (eased pitch and bank transitions), so draw mode still reads as a smooth curve through the points. This is the RCT-style compromise that keeps track deterministic, collidable and buildable piece by piece; free-form 3D splines are not in this plan.
- Piece set: station (straight, 1 tile, flat), straight (1 tile), curve left/right small (2x2 tiles, quarter turn) and large (3x3), slope transitions flat→gentle, gentle→flat, gentle→steep, steep→gentle (up and down), straight gentle and steep slopes, banked curves (bank in/out transitions plus banked small/large curves), lift hill (any up-slope with a chain flag), brakes (flat straight with a brake flag). Every piece has an exact entry and exit pose; a table in `packages/content` defines each piece's segments.
- One train per coaster in this plan (block sections and multiple trains: Plan E2). A train is 2–6 cars of 4 riders (family default 4 cars).
- A coaster must be a closed circuit containing exactly one station run of 2–8 station pieces, at least one lift or launch-free drop from the station's height, and must pass a test run before it can open.
- Ratings: excitement, intensity, nausea on the 0..999 scale, from max speed, total drop, number of drops, airtime (vertical G below 0.2), max vertical and lateral G, ride time, and an intensity penalty above 6.50; tuned so a sensible first coaster lands near excitement 5–6 and a reckless one is flagged too intense.
- Costs: per piece from the family (lift and brake pieces cost more), charged when built, half refunded on removal.

## Global Constraints

- Everything in Plans A–D2 holds: determinism in `packages/sim`, `packages/content`, `packages/protocol` (integer and fixed-point math only — trig from integer tables in content; no floats in sim state; ordered iteration); every mutation a logged, undoable command (except `sim_*`/`save_*`); snake_case names; conventional commits with the trailers used on this branch; the full verification green at every pushed commit: `pnpm format && pnpm typecheck && pnpm build && pnpm test && pnpm format:check && pnpm e2e`, plus `PERF=1 pnpm vitest run packages/sim/test/perf.test.ts` and `bash scripts/demo-headless.sh`; golden scenario extended for every new command with the hash re-recorded and noted, and the same under Bun.
- Save schema bumps with a fixture captured from the unchanged code first; protocol bumps refuse older peers.
- All art original and procedural; generic names; no imitation of any existing game's track styles, colours or names.
- Software render tier stays cheap: a coaster's track at low under 6,000 triangles per 100 pieces, supports merged, no shadows at low; trains instanced; e2e grows by at most ~30 s.
- Nothing in `apps/web` or `packages/render` computes physics or track validity; they draw, place and query.

## Review Focus

1. Pieces join exactly: after any sequence of pieces the end pose equals the sum of piece deltas, every piece's sampled segments start and end on its declared poses, and a closed circuit closes to the exact station pose (no drift), at every rotation. Test in Task 1.
2. Track can never pass through terrain, water, paths, other rides, other track or the park edge; supports never stand on water or paths; removing or undoing pieces restores occupancy exactly. Test in Task 2.
3. Physics is deterministic and stable: the same track gives the same test stats on V8 and JSC; a train that cannot crest a hill is detected as stalled (and rolls back to settle) rather than looping forever; brakes and the station always bring a train to rest in the station. Test in Task 4.
4. Guests are never lost or duplicated by trains: closing, demolishing or editing a coaster with a train out unloads riders at the station exit (or nearest path); a ride that fails a test cannot open. Test in Task 5.
5. `.track` files round-trip exactly and are refused (with a reason) when the family is unknown, the shape is not closed, or it does not fit where it is placed. Test in Task 6.
6. Point-driven building never produces a track the piece rules would refuse, and explains each refused point. Test in Task 3.

- Test: `packages/sim/test/coaster-build.test.ts`

**Interfaces:**

Every command undoable; undo of a piece restores occupancy exactly.
- Queries: `coaster_get { id }` (pieces, poses, complete, tested, stats), `coaster_piece_options { id }` (which pieces may come next and why each other is refused), `content_list` includes families and pieces.
